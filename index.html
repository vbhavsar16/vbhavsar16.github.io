Selected research

Measuring AI reliability

I study how to trace claims to evidence, catch errors before they propagate, and route uncertain decisions to human reviewers.

Workflow evaluation · Manuscript in progressTRACE 100% → 18.8%Downstream fault propagation

TRACE evaluates legal agents as deterministic workflows over typed, source-linked artifacts. It combines claim-level citation checks, tamper-evident logs, and staged attorney approval.

I withdrew TRACE from AAAI 2027 and plan to submit a revised manuscript to another conference.

In a preregistered fault-injection study, automated verification reduced downstream fault propagation from 100% to 18.8% (p = .008).

I developed the Hallucination Risk Score and Legal Evidence Reliance Score to assess evidence support, citation support, parametric drift, and retrieval weakness at the level of individual claims.

Validation covered 250 answers and 1,438 claims. Route-aware retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) cut measured hallucination risk by 38% at 16% lower cost, and reranked hybrid retrieval improved Hit@k from 0.78 to 0.96.

I built a hierarchical MPNet classifier for 11 document types and six fraud subtypes. Selective abstention routes uncertain classifications to human review before the evidence reaches downstream models.

On leakage-controlled grouped splits, it achieved 0.96 macro-F1 and 0.028 expected calibration error at 98.6% coverage. Its ONNX export preserved top-1 predictions exactly.
